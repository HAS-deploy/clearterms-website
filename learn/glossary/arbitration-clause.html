arbitration clause — what it means in a contract

What it means

An arbitration clause is a provision in a contract requiring that disputes between the parties be resolved through arbitration rather than through the court system. By agreeing to it, both parties give up their right to sue each other in court and instead submit any disagreements to a neutral third party (an arbitrator or panel of arbitrators) whose decision is typically binding and final.

Most arbitration clauses also specify procedural details: which arbitration body administers the process (such as the American Arbitration Association or JAMS), where hearings take place, how many arbitrators are used, and which state's law governs. Some clauses go further and waive the right to participate in class-action lawsuits, a particularly significant restriction.

Why it matters

Arbitration clauses appear constantly in everyday contracts — employment agreements, credit card terms, consumer product warranties, apartment leases, and app terms of service. When you sign a contract with one, you're agreeing in advance that if something goes wrong, you cannot take the dispute to court. Arbitration is generally faster and cheaper than litigation, which benefits both parties in smaller commercial disputes. However, critics argue it systematically favors businesses: proceedings are private, arbitrators may have repeat-business incentives with corporate clients, and losing parties have very limited grounds to appeal a bad decision.

For businesses drafting contracts, a well-written arbitration clause reduces litigation exposure and controls where and how disputes are handled. For individuals, encountering one often means accepting it or walking away from the deal entirely.

Example

A software company includes this in its user agreement: "Any dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall be resolved by binding arbitration administered by the AAA under its Commercial Arbitration Rules, with proceedings held in Delaware." A user who later believes the company misused their data cannot file a lawsuit — they must bring their claim through AAA arbitration in Delaware, and the outcome will be binding.

Common confusions

  • Arbitration vs. mediation: Mediation involves a neutral facilitator who helps parties reach a voluntary settlement. An arbitrator actually decides the outcome, like a private judge. An arbitration clause does not require mediation.
  • Mandatory vs. optional arbitration: Some contracts allow arbitration as one option among several; others make it the only option. The word "shall" or "must" signals mandatory arbitration.
  • Arbitration clause vs. forum selection clause: A forum selection clause specifies which court has jurisdiction. An arbitration clause removes the dispute from courts altogether. Contracts sometimes include both, governing different types of disputes.
  • Binding vs. non-binding arbitration: Most arbitration clauses specify binding arbitration, meaning the decision is final and enforceable like a court judgment. Non-binding arbitration exists but is far less common in standard contracts.
