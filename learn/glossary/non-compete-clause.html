non-compete clause — what it means in a contract

What it means

A non-compete clause is a contractual provision that restricts one party — usually an employee or a business seller — from working for competitors or starting a competing business for a defined period of time within a defined geographic area after the relationship ends. The goal is to protect legitimate business interests such as trade secrets, client relationships, and confidential information from being used against the party that invested in developing them.

These clauses must typically be reasonable in scope, duration, and geography to be enforceable. Courts in many jurisdictions will refuse to enforce a non-compete that is overly broad, and some states (notably California, North Dakota, and Minnesota) effectively ban them for employees altogether.

Why it matters

Non-competes appear most often in three situations: employment agreements (especially for senior staff, salespeople, or those with access to proprietary information), business sale agreements (where the seller agrees not to immediately open a competing shop), and partnership or shareholder agreements. If you sign one and later take a job at a rival company or launch a competing venture, you could face an injunction that forces you to stop work immediately, as well as a lawsuit for damages. Employers sometimes use them as leverage even when the clause may not hold up in court, so knowing your state's rules is essential before signing.

Example

A software engineer signs an employment contract with a cybersecurity firm. Buried in the agreement is a non-compete clause stating she cannot work for any cybersecurity company within 100 miles for two years after leaving. When she resigns to join a competitor across town, the former employer threatens legal action. Whether that clause holds depends heavily on her state's law and whether the restriction is considered reasonable for her role.

Common confusions

Non-compete vs. non-solicitation clause: A non-solicitation clause only prevents you from poaching the company's clients or employees — it doesn't stop you from working in the same industry. These are narrower and more commonly enforced.

Non-compete vs. non-disclosure agreement (NDA): An NDA prevents you from sharing confidential information but places no restriction on where you work. You can join a direct competitor and still honor an NDA as long as you don't disclose protected information.

"Unenforceable" doesn't mean "safe to ignore": Even if a non-compete is likely unenforceable in your jurisdiction, violating it can still trigger costly litigation. Many employees comply or negotiate a release rather than fight it in court.
