assignment clause — what it means in a contract

What it means

An assignment clause is a contract provision that controls whether — and how — a party can transfer their rights or obligations under the agreement to someone else. "Assigning" a contract means handing off your position in it to a third party, who then steps into your shoes. Assignment clauses typically either permit free assignment, require the other party's consent before any transfer, or prohibit assignment entirely.

Most commercial contracts include some version of this clause because, without it, the default rules under contract law (which vary by jurisdiction) govern what's allowed — and those defaults are often unclear or inconvenient for the parties involved.

Why it matters

Assignment clauses become critical in several common situations:

  • Business acquisitions and mergers: When a company is sold, the buyer needs to know whether existing contracts transfer automatically or require counterparty consent. A strict anti-assignment clause can block a deal or require renegotiating dozens of contracts.
  • Outsourcing or restructuring: If a company wants to shift contractual duties to a subsidiary or new entity, an assignment clause determines whether that's permitted.
  • Landlord-tenant relationships: Leases routinely restrict a tenant's ability to sublease or assign the lease to a new occupant.
  • Service agreements: A client may have chosen a vendor specifically for their expertise; an assignment clause prevents that vendor from quietly handing the work off to someone else.

Overlooking an assignment clause during due diligence is a common and costly mistake — a contract can become void or trigger a breach claim if it's transferred without required consent.

Example

A software company signs a five-year SaaS agreement with a client that includes this language: "Neither party may assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other party." The software company is later acquired. The acquiring company cannot simply assume the SaaS agreement — it must first obtain written consent from the client. If it proceeds without consent, the client may have grounds to terminate the contract.

Common confusions

Assignment vs. delegation: Assignment transfers rights (e.g., the right to receive payment); delegation transfers duties (e.g., the obligation to perform work). Many clauses cover both, but they're technically distinct concepts. Delegating duties doesn't relieve the original party of liability unless the other side expressly agrees.

Assignment vs. novation: A novation completely replaces one party with a new one, releasing the original party from all obligations. An assignment doesn't automatically release the assignor — they may remain on the hook if the assignee fails to perform. If a clean exit is the goal, a novation agreement is the right tool, not a simple assignment.
