non-disclosure agreement — what it means in a contract

What it means

A non-disclosure agreement (NDA) is a legally binding contract in which one or more parties agree to keep certain information confidential and not share it with outside parties. The information protected is typically defined in the agreement itself and can include trade secrets, business strategies, financial data, technical specifications, or any other proprietary information. NDAs can be unilateral (one party shares, the other keeps quiet) or mutual (both parties share sensitive information and both are bound to confidentiality).

Breaking an NDA can expose the breaching party to significant legal consequences, including injunctions to stop further disclosure and damages for any harm caused by the breach.

Why it matters

NDAs come up constantly in business and employment contexts. Employers routinely ask new hires to sign them before sharing internal processes, client lists, or product plans. Companies exchange NDAs before merger talks, partnership negotiations, or vendor discussions where proprietary information must be shared to evaluate the deal. Inventors and entrepreneurs use them when pitching ideas to potential investors or developers. In litigation, NDAs (or confidentiality agreements) are commonly attached to settlement agreements to prevent parties from discussing terms publicly.

The enforceability of an NDA depends on factors like how specifically the confidential information is defined, the duration of the obligation, and whether the restriction is reasonable in scope. Overly broad NDAs — ones that try to restrict publicly available information or last indefinitely — may be unenforceable in some jurisdictions.

Example

A software startup is in talks with a larger company about a potential acquisition. Before sharing its source code, revenue figures, and customer data, the startup asks the larger company to sign an NDA. The agreement specifies that all shared materials are confidential, prohibits the larger company from using that information to compete, and sets the obligation to last three years. If the acquisition falls through, the larger company cannot use the startup's proprietary data for its own benefit.

Common confusions

NDA vs. confidentiality clause: An NDA is a standalone contract dedicated entirely to confidentiality. A confidentiality clause is a single provision embedded inside a broader contract (like an employment agreement or services contract). They serve the same purpose but differ in form.

NDA vs. non-compete agreement: These are often signed together but are distinct. An NDA restricts sharing information; a non-compete restricts working for competitors or starting a competing business. Signing one does not mean you've signed the other.

"Mutual" vs. "one-way" NDAs: People sometimes sign a mutual NDA assuming it protects their own information, without realizing that they are simultaneously obligated to protect the other party's information — including information they may not have expected to receive.
