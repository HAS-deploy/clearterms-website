mutual rescission — what it means in a contract

What it means

Mutual rescission is an agreement between two contracting parties to cancel their contract and return to the position they were in before the contract existed. Both sides agree to walk away, releasing each other from any remaining obligations. Because it is mutual, neither party is acting unilaterally — the cancellation itself is a new agreement supported by the consideration of each party giving up their rights under the original contract.

The result is as if the original contract never happened. Any money exchanged is typically returned, work performed may need to be compensated or unwound, and neither party can later sue the other for obligations that existed under the cancelled contract.

Why it matters

Mutual rescission comes up whenever a deal goes sideways but neither side wants litigation. Common scenarios include:

  • A buyer and seller agreeing to cancel a real estate purchase before closing when financing falls through
  • A client and contractor agreeing to end a service agreement mid-project when the scope has changed beyond recognition
  • Two businesses calling off a supply arrangement that no longer makes commercial sense for either side

Because mutual rescission extinguishes the contract, it is important that the rescission agreement itself is written clearly. If it is informal or ambiguous, one party might later claim the contract is still in effect and demand performance or damages.

Example

A software company signs a one-year development contract with a client. Four months in, the client's business pivots and the software is no longer needed. Rather than litigate over the remaining eight months of fees, both parties sign a mutual rescission agreement. The client pays for the work completed so far, the developer hands over the code produced, and both parties release each other from any further claims. The original contract is treated as ended.

Common confusions

Mutual rescission vs. termination for convenience. A termination clause lets one party exit under predefined conditions — it is unilateral. Mutual rescission requires both parties to agree at the time of cancellation.

Mutual rescission vs. breach. If one party simply stops performing, that is a breach, not rescission. The non-breaching party may then have a right to rescind, but that is a legal remedy, not mutual agreement.

Mutual rescission vs. novation. Novation replaces one contract (or one party) with a new arrangement. Mutual rescission simply ends the contract without substituting anything in its place.

Rescission vs. termination generally. Rescission treats the contract as void from the beginning and aims to restore the original positions of the parties. Termination ends the contract going forward but typically preserves rights and obligations that already accrued.
